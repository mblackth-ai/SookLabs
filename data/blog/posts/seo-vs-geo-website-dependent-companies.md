SEO vs GEO is a job split, not a rebrand. SEO still wins crawlable pages, internal links, and the blue-link inventory Google and Bing can rank. GEO (generative engine optimisation) wins whether AI answer engines can cite you accurately when a buyer asks a question without clicking ten results. Most website-dependent companies need both. The useful decision is which job is failing first, not which acronym sounds newer.

This guide is for operators who depend on inbound from a website: agencies, SaaS, local services, and specialist retailers. It is not a claim that one tactic replaces the other, and it is not a ranking guarantee.

## What buyers usually mean by SEO vs GEO

People search this comparison because search behaviour split. Classic SEO still matters for category pages, comparisons, and branded queries. GEO matters because AI overviews, chat assistants, and answer engines summarise the market before a click happens.

The confusion starts when teams treat GEO as “SEO with extra keywords.” That usually produces longer blog posts that still cannot be cited, because the page never answers the question in the first paragraph, never states a decision, and never matches a stable public fact the model can reuse.

SEO work improves **discoverability in a results list**. GEO work improves **citability in an answer**. Those are related surfaces. They are not the same KPI.

## The job each system is supposed to do

A useful comparison starts with the job, the way a store fixture comparison starts with the merchandise rather than the panel colour.

| Job | SEO is usually the better system | GEO is usually the better system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get a URL into a results list | Yes | Indirectly |
| Win a comparison query with a dedicated page | Yes | Yes, if the page answers first |
| Be quoted in an AI overview | Sometimes | That is the primary job |
| Keep FAQs, services, and pricing consistent | Supporting | Required |
| Recover from a thin or duplicated site | Technical SEO first | Content truth first |

If the site cannot be crawled, indexed, or internally linked, GEO work is theatre. If the site ranks but AI answers describe a competitor — or invent your offer — GEO work is the missing system.

## When SEO is the better first move

Choose SEO-first when the site still fails basic retrieval.

Typical signals:

- Important URLs are missing from `sitemap.xml` or blocked in `robots.txt`
- Service pages cannibalise each other
- The blog is a thin recap feed with no decision titles
- Internal links point at campaign landing pages that expire
- Title tags and H1s do not match the query the page claims to serve

SEO-first work is unglamorous: indexation, canonicals, internal links, one page per job, and honest metadata. SookLabs treats that as outgoing control work inside [SEOS](/audit) — diagnostics and content systems — not as a slogan on the homepage.

## When GEO is the better first move

Choose GEO-first when retrieval already works and citation does not.

Typical signals:

- You rank for the brand and a few money terms, but AI answers omit you
- Models quote an old service list, a dead price, or a competitor’s positioning
- FAQs live in a chatbot, a CMS, and a PDF, and they disagree
- The about page is marketing copy with no extractable facts
- There is no `llms.txt`, no stable organisation markup, and no public Q&A that matches what sales actually says

GEO-first work is also unglamorous: one canonical fact set, pages that answer in the lead, FAQ blocks a model can lift, and machine-readable summaries that match the visible page. ## Why “blog more” fails both jobs

## A practical sequencing rule

Use this order unless a diagnostic says otherwise:

1. **Make the site retrievable.** Crawl, sitemap, canonicals, one URL per job.
2. **Make the facts stable.** Services, hours, offers, and definitions in one place.
3. **Make the answers extractable.** Direct-answer leads, FAQs, comparison tables.
4. **Make the machines a copy of the humans.** `llms.txt` and schema that match the page, not an invented graph.
5. **Only then scale publishing.** More URLs help when each URL earns its index slot.

Skipping to step 5 is how teams get 80 posts and zero citations.

## What “good enough” looks like for each system

SEO is good enough when:

- Money pages are indexable and internally linked
- Comparison and guide URLs match real queries
- Cannibalisation is rare
- Metadata describes the page that exists

GEO is good enough when:

- A stranger can quote your offer from the first paragraph
- FAQ answers match what staff actually say
- Public machine files do not contradict the site
- AI answers that mention you are directionally true

Neither is “done.” Both are operating systems. SEOS is the outgoing control product for that work; it is still **In progress**, which is an honest status, not a Connected badge.

## Common mistakes when teams pick a side

**Treating GEO as keyword stuffing for ChatGPT.** Models do not need more synonyms. They need a clear decision, a date, and a fact they can reuse.

**Deleting SEO because AI overviews exist.** Overviews still sit on top of a web of pages. If you are not in that web, you are not in the summary.

**Building a chatbot knowledge database separate from the site.** That is how answers drift. Sookly should consume the canonical Knowledge Base, not invent a third script.

**Publishing news recaps.** Rewritten wire copy does not create a commercial path. Reframe the news into an operator decision, then link the product that actually does the job.

## What a comparison page must contain to serve both jobs

The Retail Display USA pattern is the metric: the title is the query, the first paragraph is the answer, and the headings are the follow-up questions a buyer would actually ask. Apply that here.

A visibility comparison URL should include:

- A one-sentence decision in the lead
- A table that splits jobs, not slogans
- Explicit “when A / when B” sections
- A commercial path to a real page (audit, product, resource) rather than a newsletter signup
- An FAQ whose answers can be lifted without the rest of the essay

If a page cannot do that, it is not ready to scale. Publishing ten more URLs will not fix a page that never decides.

Internal links should go to money pages and sibling guides, not to expired campaigns. ## FAQ

### Is GEO replacing SEO in 2026?

No. GEO does not replace crawl, index, and link structure. It adds a citability job on top of pages that already need to be retrievable. Teams that abandon SEO usually disappear from both lists and answers.

### Can I do GEO without a blog?

Yes. Service pages, FAQs, and a factual organisation page can be enough for citation. A blog helps when each post targets a real decision query and links back to money pages. Volume without structure does not.

### Should llms.txt come before schema.org?

Start with whichever is missing and cheaper to make truthful. A short `llms.txt` that matches the site is more useful than a large JSON-LD graph that invents departments. Many companies need both. See the [Resources hub](/resources) for manual starters.

### How does this relate to SookLabs products?

SEOS is outgoing visibility work. Sookly is incoming conversation control. RoastMyOpSec is exposure checking. The public blog is organic proof of the content system, not a second source of business truth.

### What is the fastest way to see which job is failing?

Check three things: can Google list the money URLs, can an AI assistant describe the offer accurately, and can a lead actually reach a human. The first is SEO. The second is GEO. The third is inbound.

### Do I need to publish daily for GEO?

No. Cadence only helps after each URL has a job. A weekly comparison guide that answers in the lead will beat a daily recap that never decides.
